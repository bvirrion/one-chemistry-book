the chapter list of record; the run's rulings are in

Rules this outline already obeys:

- **Pure chemistry.** States of matter, changes of state, density, heat,
  radioactivity and nuclear energy belong to *One Physics Book*. Where
  chemistry needs one of them (a melting point used to identify a substance,
  a gas volume), the chapter uses it as known and does not teach it.
- **Book 1 never repeats itself.** Every notion has exactly one **owner**
  chapter, the earliest grade where it fits. A later chapter that needs it
  opens with a 3–5 line *You already know* recall box pointing to the owner,
  and only adds what is new (see the ownership map at the end).
- **Years 1–3 together cover the full licence**, with the prépa programmes
  inside it; no notion is owned by two university books. A university chapter
  may re-derive a Book 1 notion rigorously (that is what the first year of
  university does), but never re-teaches it at the Book 1 level.
- Year 2 is written at PC\* depth; Year 3 is balanced between physical,
  inorganic, organic and analytical chemistry.
- Each university book has one lab-techniques chapter; lab procedures appear
  in *In the lab* boxes. **No experiment is ever proposed for home.**
- Printed text never names a French programme or a university; provenance
  lives in `sources/outline_sources.md` and in source comments only.

Chapter slugs below are the future file names (`parts/<year>/NN-<slug>.tex`)
and label stems (`ch:<g7|b1…>:<slug>`).

## Book 1 — Grades 1–12

### Grade 1
1. **What Things Are Made Of** — `materials-around-us`
   Objects versus the materials they are made of (wood, metal, glass, plastic,
   paper, fabric, stone); sorting materials by simple properties (hard, soft,
   transparent, attracted by a magnet, floats).

### Grade 2
1. **Mixing and Dissolving** — `mixing-and-dissolving`
   Mixing things together; some solids disappear in water (sugar, salt) and
   some do not (sand, chalk); a solution still contains what was dissolved
   (taste, evaporation leaves it behind).

### Grade 3
1. **Separating Mixtures** — `separating-mixtures`
   Sorting by hand, sieving, letting settle (decanting), filtering,
   evaporating; which method for which mixture; how dirty water is made clean
   step by step.

### Grade 4
1. **Air, a Mixture of Gases** — `air-a-mixture-of-gases`
   Air is matter and a mixture: about four fifths nitrogen, one fifth oxygen,
   a little carbon dioxide and water vapour; what we breathe in and out.
2. **Burning: What a Fire Needs** — `what-a-fire-needs`
   Fuel, oxygen and heat (the fire triangle); burning makes new substances
   (smoke, ash, carbon dioxide, water); how fires are put out; fire safety.

### Grade 5
1. **Changes That Cannot Be Undone** — `irreversible-changes`
   Cooking an egg, baking, rusting, burning, milk turning sour: new substances
   appear; contrasted with mixing and dissolving, which can be undone.
2. **From Raw Materials to Recycling** — `raw-materials-and-recycling`
   Natural and manufactured materials, where they come from (wood, ore, oil,
   sand), how they are transformed, sorted and recycled; waste and resources.

### Grade 6
1. **Pure Substances and Mixtures** — `pure-substances-and-mixtures`
   Chemical species; a pure substance versus a mixture; homogeneous and
   heterogeneous mixtures; constants that identify a pure substance (melting
   and boiling temperature, density, used as data, taught in physics).
2. **Solutions and Solubility** — `solutions-and-solubility`
   Solute, solvent, solution; saturation and solubility in grams per litre;
   miscible and immiscible liquids; gases dissolve too (carbon dioxide in
   water, fizzy drinks, oceans).

### Grade 7
1. **Identifying Substances** — `identifying-substances`
   Characteristic tests (limewater for carbon dioxide, anhydrous copper
   sulfate for water, glowing splint for oxygen, the pop test for hydrogen);
   paper chromatography of dyes; reading a test bank.
2. **Atoms and Molecules** — `atoms-and-molecules`
   Atoms as the building blocks, molecules as groups of atoms; symbols and
   chemical formulas (O₂, H₂, N₂, H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O); molecular models.
3. **Chemical Reactions: Reactants and Products** — `chemical-reactions`
   A chemical transformation versus a physical one; reactants and products;
   a reaction as a rearrangement of atoms.

### Grade 8
1. **Conservation of Mass and Balanced Equations** — `balanced-equations`
   Mass is conserved because atoms are; writing and balancing an equation;
   reading an equation in molecules.
2. **Combustion: Fuels, Products and Greenhouse Gases** — `combustion-and-fuels`
   Combustion of carbon and methane, complete and incomplete; carbon monoxide
   danger; carbon dioxide and the greenhouse effect; fossil and renewable fuels.
3. **Plastics and Synthetic Materials** — `plastics`
   Natural, artificial and synthetic materials; the main plastics and their
   uses; recycling codes; plastics in the environment.

### Grade 9
1. **Inside the Atom** — `inside-the-atom`
   Nucleus (protons, neutrons) and electrons; atomic number, mass number,
   isotopes; sizes and masses compared; the history of the atomic model.
2. **Ions and Ionic Solutions** — `ions`
   Atoms that gain or lose electrons; formulas of ions and ionic compounds;
   ionic solutions conduct; precipitation tests for common ions.
3. **Acids, Bases and pH** — `acids-bases-ph`
   The H⁺ and OH⁻ ions; the pH scale and its measurement; diluting an acid;
   everyday acids and bases; safety with corrosive products.
4. **Metals: Reactions with Acids and Corrosion** — `metals-acids-corrosion`
   Iron, zinc and aluminium with hydrochloric acid (hydrogen and metal ions);
   corrosion of metals; protecting metals.
5. **The Periodic Table: A First Look** — `periodic-table-first-look`
   Elements, symbols, atomic number; rows and columns; metals and non-metals;
   Mendeleev's idea.
6. **Elements in the Universe and on Earth** — `elements-universe-earth`
   Which elements are abundant in the Universe, the Earth, the oceans and the
   human body; elements made in stars (told, the physics is in the physics
   book); the same atoms recycled through the ages.

### Grade 10
1. **Chemical Species, Natural and Synthetic** — `chemical-species`
   Extracting a species from a natural product; synthesising the identical
   species; thin-layer chromatography and the retention factor.
2. **Electron Shells and the Periodic Table** — `electron-shells`
   Electron configuration (1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p); valence electrons; families of
   the periodic table; stable ions and the noble-gas rule.
3. **Lewis Structures and the Shape of Molecules** — `lewis-and-shape`
   The covalent bond; bonding and lone pairs; Lewis structures; shapes of
   simple molecules (linear, bent, trigonal, tetrahedral).
4. **The Mole and Molar Mass** — `the-mole`
   The amount of substance; the Avogadro constant; molar mass; from mass to
   moles and back.
5. **Concentration and Dilution** — `concentration-and-dilution`
   Mass and molar concentration; preparing a solution by dissolving or by
   diluting; a calibration scale.
6. **The Reaction-Progress Table** — `reaction-progress-table`
   The extent of a reaction; the progress table; limiting reactant;
   stoichiometric mixtures; final state of a total reaction.
7. **Synthesis: Yield and Purity** — `synthesis-yield`
   Steps of a synthesis (reaction, isolation, purification, analysis);
   heating under reflux, filtration, recrystallisation; yield.

### Grade 11
1. **Colour and Absorbance** — `absorbance`
   Colour of a solution and its absorption spectrum; absorbance; the
   Beer–Lambert law; finding a concentration from a calibration line.
2. **Electronegativity, Polarity and Intermolecular Forces** — `polarity-and-cohesion`
   Electronegativity; polar bonds and polar molecules; van der Waals forces
   and hydrogen bonds; cohesion of molecular and ionic solids.
3. **Dissolving Ionic and Molecular Solids** — `dissolution`
   Dissolving an ionic solid (dissociation, solvation, dispersion); polarity
   and solubility; liquid–liquid extraction; soaps and amphiphilic molecules.
4. **Organic Molecules: Skeletons and Names** — `organic-skeletons`
   Molecular, semi-structural and skeletal formulas; carbon chains; naming
   alkanes; constitutional isomers.
5. **Functional Groups and Families** — `functional-groups`
   Alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, amides,
   halogenoalkanes, alkenes; naming them.
6. **Infrared Spectroscopy** — `infrared`
   Bonds vibrate and absorb infrared light; reading an IR spectrum;
   identifying functional groups.
7. **Oxidation and Reduction** — `redox`
   Oxidants and reductants; redox couples; half-equations; balancing a redox
   equation; common oxidants and reductants.
8. **Titration** — `titration`
   Equivalence; titration followed by a colour change; computing an unknown
   concentration; the titration as a measurement.
9. **The Energy of Reactions: Combustion and Bond Energies** — `reaction-energy`
   Exothermic and endothermic reactions; energy released by a combustion;
   bond energies; comparing fuels.

### Grade 12
1. **Proton NMR** — `proton-nmr`
   Chemical shift, equivalent protons, integration, the n+1 rule; using IR and
   NMR together to identify a molecule.
2. **Stereochemistry: Chirality and Isomers** — `stereochemistry`
   Chirality and asymmetric carbons; enantiomers and diastereomers; Z/E
   isomers; conformations; why stereochemistry matters in biology and drugs.
3. **Reaction Mechanisms: Curly Arrows** — `curly-arrows`
   Electron-rich and electron-poor sites; curly arrows; substitution,
   addition and elimination; elementary steps and intermediates.
4. **Reaction Rates** — `reaction-rates`
   Following a reaction in time; kinetic factors; rate of appearance and
   disappearance; first-order reactions and half-life.
5. **Catalysis** — `catalysis`
   Homogeneous, heterogeneous and enzymatic catalysis; selectivity; catalysts
   in industry and in living things.
6. **Chemical Equilibrium: Quotient and Constant** — `equilibrium`
   Non-total reactions; the reaction quotient and the equilibrium constant;
   predicting the direction of change.
7. **Acids and Bases: Ka and pKa** — `ka-and-pka`
   Brønsted acids and bases; strong and weak acids; the ionic product of
   water; Ka and pKa; pH of a strong acid or base solution.
8. **Buffers and Predominance Diagrams** — `buffers-predominance`
   Predominance and distribution diagrams; buffer solutions; acid–base
   indicators; buffers in living things.
9. **Titrations by pH and Conductivity** — `ph-conductivity-titrations`
   pH-metric titration curves and the equivalence point; conductimetric
   titration; choosing an indicator.
10. **Electrochemical Cells and Electrolysis** — `cells-and-electrolysis`
    Spontaneous redox in a cell; cell voltage, capacity; forcing a reaction
    by electrolysis; batteries, accumulators and electrolysers.
11. **Synthesis Strategy and Green Chemistry** — `synthesis-strategy`
    Optimising a synthesis (rate, yield); selectivity and protecting groups;
    atom economy; the principles of green chemistry.
12. **Polymers** — `polymers`
    Monomers, polymers, repeat units; addition and condensation polymers;
    structure and properties; natural polymers.

## Book 2 — University Year 1 (bachelor-1)

*Architecture of matter*

   Quantised energies of the atom; quantum numbers n, l, m, s; Pauli,
   Hund, Klechkowski; configurations of atoms and ions; exceptions.

   Blocks and families; trends in radius, ionisation energy, electron
   affinity; electronegativity scales (Pauling, Mulliken); polarisability.

*Transformations*

*Solutions*
10. **Acid–Base Equilibria and the Predominant-Reaction Method** — `predominant-reaction`

11. **Precipitation and Solubility** — `precipitation`

    Electrode potential; standard potentials; the Nernst equation;
    predicting redox reactions; equilibrium constant from potentials.

    Building and reading potential–pH diagrams (water, iron, copper, zinc);

    Direct, indirect, successive and simultaneous titrations; pH-metric,

*Organic chemistry*

    CIP rules and R/S, Z/E descriptors; Newman and Cram representations;

    UV–visible and conjugation; IR in depth; ¹H NMR coupling constants and

    Inductive and mesomeric effects; carbocations, carbanions and radicals;

19. **Nucleophilic Substitution** — `nucleophilic-substitution`
    SN2 and SN1 mechanisms, kinetics and stereochemistry; effects of

    E2 and E1; Zaitsev regioselectivity; anti-periplanar stereospecificity;

21. **Organomagnesium Reagents** — `organomagnesium`
    Preparing a Grignard reagent; basicity and nucleophilicity; additions to
    carbonyl compounds and carbon dioxide; building carbon skeletons.
22. **Alcohols: Activating the OH Group** — `alcohol-activation`
    Alcoholates and the Williamson synthesis; sulfonate esters; conversion to
    halides; acid-catalysed dehydration and ether formation.
23. **Carbonyls: Nucleophilic Additions, Acetals and Protection** — `acetals-protection`
    Electrophilic activation of the carbonyl; acetal and hemiacetal formation
    (Dean–Stark); protecting and deprotecting groups.
24. **Oxidation and Reduction in Organic Chemistry** — `organic-redox`
    Oxidation levels of carbon; oxidising alcohols; reducing carbonyls with
    hydrides (NaBH₄, LiAlH₄); selectivity.
25. **Alkenes and Alkynes: Electrophilic Additions** — `electrophilic-additions`
    Addition of HX, X₂ and water; Markovnikov regioselectivity; carbocation
    rearrangements; halonium ions and anti addition; alkynes and acetylides.

*The elements*
26. **Hydrogen and the s-Block** — `s-block`
    Hydrogen and hydrides; alkali and alkaline-earth metals: properties,
    compounds, industrial production and uses.
27. **The p-Block I: Boron, Carbon and Nitrogen Groups** — `p-block-13-15`
    Groups 13–15: boron, aluminium, carbon, silicon, nitrogen, phosphorus —
    oxides, acids, key compounds and industrial processes.

*Lab*

    Hazard classes, GHS pictograms, H and P statements, waste; measurement

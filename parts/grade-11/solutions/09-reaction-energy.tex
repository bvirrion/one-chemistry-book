\begin{solution}{exo:g11:reaction-energy:5}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:reaction-energy:12}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:reaction-energy:14}
The table gives average bond energies, while the \ce{C=O} bonds of
carbon dioxide are stronger than average; the measured values are for
liquid water, whose condensation releases more energy than the gas
reaction; and the method treats every bond as independent of its
neighbours. The estimate is still useful: it gives the right sign
(\omterm{def:g11:reaction-energy:exothermic}{exothermic}) and the right order of magnitude without any measurement.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:g11:reaction-energy:15}
Broken: $4 \times 464 = \qty{1856}{kJ}$; formed: $2 \times 436 + 498 =
\qty{1370}{kJ}$; $E_r \approx \qty{+486}{kJ}$: \omterm{def:g11:reaction-energy:exothermic}{endothermic}, so energy
must be supplied. \omterm{def:g4:what-a-fire-needs:burning}{Burning} the hydrogen later gives this energy back:
hydrogen stores energy produced elsewhere, it does not create any.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:g11:reaction-energy:1}
\textbf{1.} \ce{CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O}.

\textbf{2.} \ce{C2H6O + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O}.

\textbf{3.} \ce{2C8H18 + 25O2 -> 16CO2 + 18H2O}.

\textbf{4.} \ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}.

\textbf{5.} Hydrogen.

\textbf{6.} \qty{16.0}{g/mol}, \qty{46.0}{g/mol}, \qty{114.0}{g/mol},
\qty{2.0}{g/mol}.

\textbf{7.} In \si{kJ} per \si{kg}, then \si{MJ/kg}: methane
$890.7 / 0.0160$, \qty{55.7}{MJ/kg}; ethanol $1367.6 / 0.0460$,
\qty{29.7}{MJ/kg}; octane $5470 / 0.1140$, \qty{48.0}{MJ/kg}; hydrogen
$285.8 / 0.0020$, \qty{142.9}{MJ/kg}.

\textbf{8.} Hydrogen > methane > octane > ethanol.

\textbf{9.} Hydrogen is a very light gas: a kilogram of it fills a huge
volume at normal pressure, so it must be squeezed into heavy
high-pressure tanks.

\textbf{10.} Methane: 4 \ce{C-H}; dioxygen: 2 \ce{O=O} broken. Carbon
dioxide: 2 \ce{C=O}; water: $2 \times 2 = 4$ \ce{O-H} formed.

\textbf{11.} $4 \times 415 + 2 \times 498 = \qty{2656}{kJ}$.

\textbf{12.} $2 \times 741 + 4 \times 464 = \qty{3338}{kJ}$.

\textbf{13.} $E_r \approx 2656 - 3338 = \qty{-682}{kJ}$, against
\qty{-890.7}{kJ} measured: about \qty{23}{\percent} too small in size.

\textbf{14.} Average bond energies (the \ce{C=O} of carbon dioxide is
stronger than average); liquid water in the measurement, gases in the
estimate.

\textbf{15.} $1000 / 890.7 = \qty{1.123}{mol}$.

\textbf{16.} $1.123 \times 44.0 = \qty{49.4}{g}$.

\textbf{17.} Ethanol: $1000 / 1367.6 = \qty{0.731}{mol}$, giving
\qty{1.462}{mol} of carbon dioxide, \qty{64.3}{g}. Octane:
$1000 / 5470 = \qty{0.183}{mol}$, giving \qty{1.463}{mol}, \qty{64.4}{g}.

\textbf{18.} Methane: it has the most hydrogen \omterm{def:g7:atoms-and-molecules:atom}{atoms} per carbon \omterm{def:g7:atoms-and-molecules:atom}{atom} (4
against 2.25 for octane), and \omterm{def:g4:what-a-fire-needs:burning}{burning} hydrogen gives energy without
carbon dioxide.

\textbf{19.} On how the hydrogen is made: from water with electricity
from renewable sources, it releases almost no carbon dioxide; made from
methane, it releases carbon dioxide at the factory instead of the
exhaust.

\textbf{20.} Methane releases about \qty{49}{g} of carbon dioxide per
megajoule.
\end{solution}
